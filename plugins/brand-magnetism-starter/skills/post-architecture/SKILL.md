# The Post Architecture

## What This Is

This is the structural formula for writing LinkedIn posts that stop the scroll, build emotional momentum, and move people to act. It was extracted from Ngozi Cadmus's viral LinkedIn posts and validated against industry frameworks (Sam Szuchan's hook templates, Charlie Hills & Ayesha Ameer's post structures, Lara Acosta's rehook strategy, Casey Brown's content methods).

The architecture follows what the industry teaches — but adds three things the industry hasn't figured out yet:

1. **The Entry Point Strategy** — a deliberate choice between broad and niche entry that the industry only teaches one side of
2. **The Moment hook** — news and culture jacking through your own lens, which mainstream B2B hook templates completely miss
3. **The Empowerment Turn** — a distinct stage in the Deliver phase where you flip from pain to power, which is the engine of shareability and the thing no industry framework explicitly teaches

## How This Skill Relates to Other Skills

- **Post Architecture** (this skill) = the STRUCTURE of a post (how it flows from opening to close)
- **my-linkedin-voice** = the VOICE and STYLE (how it sounds, your formatting rules, your language)
- **5-day-brand-engine** = the STRATEGY (what type of post to write on which day, TOFU/MOFU/BOFU mapping)

Use all three together for the complete system.

## The Architecture: Hook → Develop → Deliver → Close

Every LinkedIn post follows four stages. This is the skeleton — the stages are always the same, but within each stage you have different moves you can choose from.

### Stage 1: HOOK (Stop the Scroll)

The opening line. Aim for 12 words or fewer (~55 characters — the LinkedIn fold) — its only job is to make someone stop scrolling and read line two. This is a strong default, not a law: a hook that sounds like the writer actually talking beats a hook that hits the word count. Speech first, structure second.

Note: personality-led posts (rants, hot takes, roast lists) may drop this arc entirely and run on conversational cadence, with the structure invisible or absent. That is intentional, not a mistake.

Before choosing your hook type, make a strategic decision about your **entry point** (see Entry Point Strategy below). Then select from these five hook types:

#### The Declaration
A bold statement that takes a stand. No question, no softness, just truth dropped with confidence.

Examples:
- "A Rested, Healed, Whole and Rich Black woman is a threat to the entire system."
- "Black women have been the world's unpaid consultants. For centuries. It stops now."
- "Black women — stop using ChatGPT as a glorified Google search."

When to use: When you have a strong opinion or truth that your audience already feels but hasn't heard someone say out loud. Declarations work best when they name something your audience is living.

#### The Reveal
A hidden truth exposed. A stat, an experiment, a finding that makes someone think "I didn't know that."

Examples:
- "It costs an extra $500,000 to be Black in America."
- "I ran a quiet experiment with Black women building their personal brands."

When to use: When you have data, research, a case study, or personal experiment results. Reveals create curiosity — people need to know what comes next.

#### The Moment
A cultural event, news story, or trending topic hijacked with your lens. This is what the industry calls trend-jacking or news-hacking, but the key is you're not just commenting on the news — you're reframing it through your expertise and worldview.

Examples:
- "Coco Gauff wins the French Open. Her opponent's response?"
- "Five Black women found dead."

When to use: When something is happening in culture that your audience cares about and you have a perspective that adds depth the mainstream conversation is missing. Best used within 24-48 hours of the event. LinkedIn gets news later than X/Twitter, so you have a wider window than you think.

#### The Flip
The opposite of what everyone expects. A contrarian take that makes someone stop because it contradicts conventional wisdom.

Examples:
- "I've deliberately stopped focusing on follower growth."
- "ChatGPT praises your worst ideas. Claude tells you the truth."

When to use: When you can challenge a widely-held belief with credibility and evidence to back it up. The Flip only works if you can prove your contrarian point — otherwise it's just clickbait.

#### The Claim
Your personal results or achievement, stated without apology. Not a humble brag — a power statement that positions you through proof.

Examples:
- "They didn't find me on a speaker list. They found me on LinkedIn."
- "10,000+ followers in 7 months. 2M+ impressions."

When to use: When you have a genuine result that demonstrates your authority. The Claim works because it's specific and verifiable. Vague claims ("I've helped hundreds of people...") are weak. Specific claims ("10,000 followers in 7 months") are magnetic.

### Stage 2: DEVELOP (Build the Case)

The body of your post. This is where you earn the reader's attention by going deeper. You can combine multiple development techniques in a single post — most viral posts use two or three together.

#### Evidence Stacking
Stats, facts, data points, and proof piled up — often using arrow (→) formatting. Each piece of evidence builds on the last until the case is undeniable.

Example (from the Juneteenth post):
"As employees: → Black workers earn $0.73 for every $1 white workers earn → Black professionals are promoted 18% less often..."
Then continues across creators, parents, homeowners, healthcare — six categories of evidence stacked.

When to use: When you're making a systemic argument or exposing a pattern. Evidence stacking works because each stat alone is concerning, but stacked together they become impossible to dismiss.

#### Rhythm Building
Repetitive parallel structures that create emotional momentum — anaphora (same opening phrase repeated), parallel sentences, rhythmic patterns that hit like a drumbeat.

Example:
"They need us tired. They need us broken. They need us struggling. They need us grateful for scraps."

And later in the same post:
"When we're rested, we think clearly. When we're healed, we set boundaries. When we're whole, we know our worth. When we're rich, we can't be controlled."

When to use: When you want to build emotional intensity. Rhythm creates a feeling of momentum — the reader gets pulled along by the pattern. It is particularly powerful for advocacy, empowerment, and identity-affirming content.

#### Story / Narrative
A real example, case study, or personal experience that illustrates the point. Not abstract — specific, named, concrete.

Example (from the Juneteenth post):
"That's what Paul and Tanisha Austin discovered when they got their home appraised twice. First appraisal with their family photos up? $989k. Second appraisal after hiding all traces of Blackness and having a white friend pretend to own their home? $1.5M."

When to use: When you have a specific story that makes the abstract concrete. Stories are how people remember — a stat tells them the problem exists, a story makes them feel it.

#### Naming the Invisible
Articulating what your audience feels but hasn't been able to say. Putting words to their lived experience. This is a signature technique — it's deeper than a "rehook" (which the industry teaches as just a punchy second line). This is an act of recognition.

Example:
"When we win, it's not because we were brilliant. It's because someone else failed. When we succeed, it's luck, not skill. When we excel, the focus shifts to wounded egos instead of our excellence."

When to use: When your audience is experiencing something they haven't seen articulated. Naming the invisible creates the most powerful reader response — "YES, that's exactly what it feels like." This technique drives saves, shares, and DMs more than any other because people send it to friends saying "you need to read this."

### Stage 3: DELIVER (The Payoff)

What the reader gets for staying. This is the return on their attention — the insight, the solution, the shift in perspective, or the moment of empowerment. The Deliver stage is where the post earns its share.

#### The Empowerment Turn
The flip from pain to power. You've built the case for what's wrong — now you show what's possible. This is the single most important technique for virality because it's the moment that makes people share. People don't share pain alone — they share pain that's been transformed into power.

Examples:
- "They told us rest was lazy. We discovered it was STRATEGIC. They said healing was selfish. We learned it was REVOLUTIONARY."
- "That's not luck. That's not someone else's mistakes. That's BRILLIANCE."
- "But our stories? Our visibility? Our refusal to be erased? That's how we change the price of admission."

This is the stage the industry frameworks miss entirely. Charlie Hills goes from key points straight to power ending. There is no explicit pivot taught anywhere in mainstream LinkedIn education. This is your competitive advantage — teach it.

#### The Solution
Steps, tips, actionable breakdown. Practical value the reader can implement.

Example (from the follower growth post):
"1. Post case study carousels to attract clients. 2. Post photos of you speaking to signal authority. 3. Get yourself featured wherever you can."

When to use: For educational and authority content (Wednesday/Thursday in the 5-Day Brand Engine). The Solution works best when it's specific and immediately actionable.

#### The Reframe
Changing how the reader sees the situation entirely. Not adding new information — shifting the lens.

Example:
"We're not picking cotton anymore. But we're still being systematically harvested — of our time, our talent, our potential, our lives. The shackles just moved from our ankles to our credit scores."

When to use: When the insight IS the value. Reframes are powerful closers for advocacy and thought leadership content because they give people new language to understand their experience.

---

### Stage 4: CLOSE (Move Them)

The final lines. Every post should end by giving the reader something to DO — with the emotion, with the information, with the inspiration. Never leave them sitting in the feeling.

#### DM Trigger
Invite readers to DM a specific keyword. Creates a direct conversation and moves people into your nurturing pipeline.

Example: "DM me 'AI ANIMATION' if you want to begin using the power of AI animation to bring your stories to life."

#### Repost Call
Ask readers to repost with a reason tied to the post's message. This extends reach and signals community alignment.

Example: "Repost this if you believe Black women's victories deserve celebration, not explanation."

#### Community Call
Rally the audience around a shared identity, movement, or moment. No specific action — just collective affirmation.

Example: "A generation of Black women who refuse to choose between rest and riches."

#### Offer Mention
Introduce a programme, event, or service as a natural extension of the post's message.

Example: "Introducing the LinkedIn Liberation Lab. This isn't just about showing up online — this is about shifting power."

#### P.S. Positioning
A one-line reminder of who you are and who you help. Subtle repositioning for anyone discovering you through shares.

Example: "P.S. Helping Black women heal from workplace trauma and build sustainable digital income through revolutionary rest and AI tools."

You can combine close types — a post might end with an Empowerment Turn (Deliver), then a Repost Call + P.S. Positioning (Close).

## The Entry Point Strategy: Broad vs Niche

This decision sits ABOVE the four-stage architecture. Before choosing your hook type, decide: am I entering broad or entering niche?

### Broad Entry
You open with something universal. Anyone scrolling would stop — the topic, the stat, the contrarian take applies widely. Then you narrow to your specific audience and perspective as the post develops.

**Why it works:** It casts a wide net. People who wouldn't normally engage with your niche content stop because the hook speaks to them too. They stay because the story pulls them in. Your reach extends beyond your core audience, which is how you grow. Crucially, broad entry positions you as the authority IN your niche by attracting people adjacent to it — people who see your expertise and recognise it applies to or resonates with their world too.

**Examples:**
- "It costs an extra $500,000 to be Black in America." — Anyone stops at that number. Then you narrow into the specific lived experience.
- "Coco Gauff wins the French Open." — Sports fans, tennis fans, anyone who saw the headlines. Then you layer your lens.
- "ChatGPT praises your worst ideas. Claude tells you the truth." — Every AI user stops. Then you direct it toward your audience.
- "I've deliberately stopped focusing on follower growth." — Every LinkedIn creator stops. Then you deliver your positioning strategy.

**When to use:** When you want to grow your reach, attract new followers, or make yourself visible to people adjacent to your niche. These are people who may not be your direct ICP but who see your authority and bring opportunities — collaborations, speaking invitations, press features, columns. Best for TOFU content (Monday/Tuesday in the 5-Day Brand Engine).

### Niche Entry
You open by speaking DIRECTLY to your audience. You name them. You call them in. The first line makes it clear who this is for. Everyone else keeps scrolling — or, more often, they stop BECAUSE of the specificity. Specificity is magnetic.

**Why it works:** Your core audience feels seen immediately. No warm-up, no waiting to find out "is this for me?" — it's for them from word one. And paradoxically, people OUTSIDE your audience are drawn in by the specificity because it feels real, raw, and authentic in a sea of generic content. Niche entry builds deeper loyalty and stronger community.

**Examples:**
- "A Rested, Healed, Whole and Rich Black woman is a threat to the entire system." — Black women in the first line. No warm-up. And it went viral far beyond Black women.
- "Black women have been the world's unpaid consultants." — Named and claimed from the jump.
- "I ran a quiet experiment with Black women building their personal brands." — Niche from sentence one.
- "Black women — stop using ChatGPT as a glorified Google search." — Direct command to a specific audience.

**When to use:** When you want to deepen loyalty, speak directly to your buyer, build community, or make your audience feel seen. Best for MOFU and BOFU content (Wednesday-Friday in the 5-Day Brand Engine).

### The Strategic Balance

**Broad entry grows your audience. Niche entry converts your audience.** You need both.

The ratio depends on where you are in your brand journey. Early on, lean broader to grow your visibility and attract people adjacent to your niche who will amplify your authority. Once you have your audience, lean niche to convert — to move people from followers to community members to clients.

## VIRAL POST MECHANICS

Every post should be engineered for virality. Viral posts are not accidents — they follow specific structural patterns. Study the mechanics below and apply them to every post. **CRITICAL: These are PATTERNS and STRUCTURES only. Never copy language, hooks, or phrasing from any creator. Use the mechanics to write original content in the user's voice.**

Each viral element works because it triggers one or more of these four things:

- **Curiosity** — the reader needs to know what comes next
- **Emotion** — the reader feels something (pride, frustration, recognition, hope)
- **Relevance** — the reader sees themselves in the content
- **Surprise** — the reader encounters something they did not expect

If a hook or body section does not trigger at least one of these four, rewrite it until it does.

### Viral Hook Patterns

The hook is the single most important element. These six patterns consistently stop the scroll. Pick the one that fits the content — do not default to the same pattern every time.

**1. Specific Result + System Tease**
Open with a concrete, measurable result. Then tease the system or method that produced it. The parenthetical on line 3 creates a "content preview" that makes the reader stay.
- Triggers: Curiosity + Relevance
- Structure: `[Specific result]. [Proof it's real, not a flex]. ([Tease of the system that produced it] ↓)`
- Example structure (not words to copy): "Someone did X. The result was Y. (Here's the system behind it ↓)"

**2. Vulnerability + Self-Call-Out**
Lead with an honest admission of failure or struggle. The self-awareness disarms the reader and builds instant trust. The turn comes when you reveal what you learned.
- Triggers: Emotion + Surprise
- Structure: `[Honest admission]. [Specific detail that makes it real]. [Turn — what changed]`

**3. Result + Generosity Gap**
State a valuable result you achieved, then immediately give away the method for free. The gap between the value and the price (free) creates tension that demands attention.
- Triggers: Curiosity + Surprise
- Structure: `[Impressive result]. [I just gave away exactly how]. [For free / what this normally costs]`

**4. Data + Urgency**
Open with a specific number, stat, or data point that creates urgency. The specificity makes it credible. The implication makes it personal.
- Triggers: Relevance + Emotion
- Structure: `[Specific data point]. [Why it matters to the reader]. [What most people are getting wrong]`

**5. Contrarian + Specific Revenue**
Challenge a commonly held belief, then back it up with specific financial proof. The contrarian angle gets attention; the revenue makes it undeniable.
- Triggers: Surprise + Curiosity
- Structure: `[Contrarian claim]. [Specific revenue or result]. [Why the conventional wisdom is wrong]`

**6. Transformation Story**
Before and after. The gap between where someone was and where they are now — especially when the transformation was fast or unexpected.
- Triggers: Emotion + Relevance
- Structure: `[Where they were]. [What changed]. [Where they are now]`

### Viral Body Patterns

The body must maintain the momentum the hook created. These patterns keep the reader scrolling:

**Numbered Tips with Personality**
Use `1/`, `2/`, `3/` formatting. Each numbered point has a bold claim followed by a specific, practical explanation. The personality comes from short editorial asides, real examples, and a conversational tone — not from emojis or exclamation marks.

**Specific Tactical Bullets**
List the unsexy, practical actions that actually produce results. No vague advice. Every line should be something the reader can do TODAY. Use `→` formatting.

**Contrast Format (🚫 / ✅)**
Show the wrong way and the right way side by side. This works because readers instantly recognise themselves in the "wrong" column and want to move to the "right" column.

**Self-Deprecation + Receipts**
Admit what you used to do wrong, then show the receipts of what happened when you changed. The vulnerability makes the proof more powerful, not less.

**Before / After Comparisons**
Show the gap between the old way and the new way. Be specific with both — vague "befores" weaken the contrast.

### Viral Close Patterns

The close determines whether the reader engages (comments, reposts, DMs) or just scrolls on.

**Punchy One-Liner**
End with a single sentence that lands like a full stop. No explanation. No softening. It should feel like the mic dropping.

**Comment Trigger**
Give the reader a specific word to comment. "Comment [WORD] and I'll send you [specific thing]." The specificity of the word makes it feel like a real exchange, not a generic CTA.

**Full Circle Tie-Back**
Return to the hook and close the loop. If you opened with a result, end by connecting that result back to the reader's potential. This creates a satisfying narrative arc that makes the whole post feel intentional.

### How to Apply These Mechanics

When writing any post:

1. **Choose 1 hook pattern** based on the content and funnel stage. TOFU posts often work best with patterns 1, 2, or 6. MOFU posts work well with 3, 4, or 5. BOFU posts can use any pattern.
2. **Choose 1-2 body patterns** that serve the content. Do not force a pattern that does not fit.
3. **Choose 1 close pattern** — and combine it with the standard engagement CTA (question + repost line).
4. **Check all four triggers** — does the post hit at least 2 of: curiosity, emotion, relevance, surprise? If not, the hook is too weak or the body is too generic. Rewrite.
5. **Never copy.** These are structures, not scripts. Every word must be original and in the user's voice.

## Industry Validation

This framework is grounded in what the LinkedIn content industry teaches, not invented from thin air. Here's how it maps:

**Hook types** cover 7 of Sam Szuchan's 11 hook templates (consolidated into 5 more efficient categories), plus add The Moment which his B2B-focused framework misses entirely.

**Post Architecture** (Hook → Develop → Deliver → Close) aligns with Charlie Hills & Ayesha Ameer's frameworks (Hook → Rehook → PAS → Key Points → Power Ending / Problem Statement → Agitation → Solution Teaser → Solution Breakdown → CTA). The difference is that this framework adds the Empowerment Turn as a distinct Deliver technique — which is the engine of shareability that industry frameworks skip.

**Entry Point Strategy** extends what Lara Acosta teaches about broad-to-narrow post architecture by adding the niche-first approach as an equally valid (and often more powerful) alternative.

The industry confirms: hooks should be 12-14 words max, clear and direct, no flowery language, aligned with audience pain points, confidence-signaling without arrogance. All of which this framework follows.

What this framework adds that the industry doesn't teach: the dual entry point as a strategic choice, The Moment hook type, the Empowerment Turn, and Naming the Invisible as a development technique.

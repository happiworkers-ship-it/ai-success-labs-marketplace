# LinkedIn Image Prompt Generator

This skill transforms LinkedIn posts into striking, scroll-stopping image prompts for AI image generators. The output is editorial illustration concepts with bold headlines and detailed scene descriptions that produce powerful, metaphor-driven visuals.

## Before You Begin: Get Brand Colors

Before generating any prompts, you need the person's brand colors. Ask:

> "Before I create your image prompts, I need your brand colors (hex codes). Please share 2-4 colors from your brand palette — for example: `#563794` `#29bdb2` `#f5f3fc`"

Do not proceed until you have at least 2 brand colors. Store them for the session.

If the person has already shared their brand colors earlier in the conversation, use those — don't ask again.

## The Process

### Step 1: Read the Post

Read the LinkedIn post carefully and identify:
- The **core tension** — what conflict, injustice, insight, or transformation is the post really about?
- The **emotional engine** — what feeling drives the post? (frustration, defiance, empowerment, grief, determination, hope)
- The **key line** — which single line or idea from the post would hit hardest as a visual?

### Step 2: Generate the Headline

The headline is NOT a summary or caption. It's a statement that could live on a protest sign, a book cover, or a billboard. It makes the viewer stop scrolling before they even read the post.

**Headline Rules:**
- Maximum 8 words (shorter is almost always better)
- Declarative, not descriptive
- Often structured as a reframe, contradiction, or confrontation
- Uses periods for punch. Not exclamation marks.
- Can use the post's own words if there's a line that already hits hard enough

**Headline patterns that work:**
- The Reframe: "Endurance is not authority."
- The Contradiction: "Good enough to cover. Never good enough to be seen."
- The Confrontation: "This is what you call calm."
- The Equation: "Invisible = broke. Visible = booked."
- The Declaration: "Black women are being locked out in real time."
- The Question: "You hold the room together — but who's holding you up?"

These are examples, not a fixed list. Let the post's message guide the headline form.

### Step 3: Design the Visual Metaphor

The image should be a **visual argument**, not a literal illustration of the topic. The goal is to make an abstract idea physically visible.

Think: "If this post's core message were a single scene, what would you see?"

**Types of visual metaphors (non-exhaustive — invent new ones as the post demands):**
- **Contrast compositions** — split-screen showing two different realities side by side
- **Containment** — glass boxes, cages, tape, walls — something trapping or silencing
- **Burden** — carrying weight others don't see, holding things up while others benefit
- **Transformation** — before/after, rising, breaking free, stepping into light
- **Confrontation** — direct eye contact with viewer, defiant posture, standing ground
- **Isolation** — alone in a crowd, overlooked, invisible in plain sight
- **Reversal** — showing the powerful as vulnerable, or the overlooked as commanding

Don't limit yourself to these. If the post calls for a visual metaphor that doesn't fit any category, create it. The metaphor should feel inevitable once you see it — like it was the only way to show this idea.

### Step 4: Write the Prompts

Generate **3 different visual concepts** for the post. Each concept should explore a different angle or tension from the post — don't just redress the same idea three times.

For each concept, provide **3 color variants** — one per brand color. Each variant uses ONE brand color as the dominant environmental tone, paired with black and white for text and structural contrast.

**Color Rules (these are critical):**
- **ONE brand color per image** as the dominant environmental/background tone
- **Black and white** for headline text, frames, structural elements, and contrast
- **Characters always have realistic, natural skin tones** — brand colors are NEVER applied to skin, never used as a tint or wash over characters
- **Character clothing** should be described in natural colors (charcoal suit, golden-yellow blazer, white shirt, dark trousers) — not in brand colors unless it works naturally
- **Text color depends on background:** white text on dark backgrounds, black text on light backgrounds

**Art Style (use consistently across all prompts):**
- Editorial illustration style
- Clean bold lines
- Graphic novel aesthetic
- Dramatic lighting and composition
- Professional art quality

**Prompt Structure:**
Each prompt should describe, in this order:
1. The overall composition type (split, single figure, close-up, scene)
2. The primary subject and what they're doing/experiencing
3. The metaphorical elements in the scene (what makes this more than just a picture)
4. Secondary characters or elements if relevant
5. The environmental color (specifying the hex code)
6. Lighting and mood
7. The explicit instruction that characters have natural realistic skin tones and brand colors apply only to environment
8. The dominant palette statement (e.g., "Dominant palette is deep purple, black, and white")

**Characters:**
- Describe characters by what the post calls for — don't default to any specific demographic unless the post's message requires it
- Always depicted with dignity, even in difficult situations
- The emotion comes from the situation and metaphor, not from diminishing the character
- Professional or smart-casual attire unless the scene calls for something specific

### Step 5: Present the Output

Format your output like this for each concept:

---

**CONCEPT [number] — "[Short concept name]"**

**Headline:** `THE HEADLINE IN CAPS`

**[Color Name] (`#hexcode`):**
`[Full image generator prompt]`

**[Color Name] (`#hexcode`):**
`[Full image generator prompt]`

**[Color Name] (`#hexcode`):**
`[Full image generator prompt]`

---

After presenting all 3 concepts with their color variants, briefly note which concept you think hits hardest for this particular post and why — but leave the choice to the person.

## Example

**Input post (summarised):** A post about Black women building credibility brick by brick while others walk into rooms and credibility is assumed. The system has a recognition problem, not a credibility problem. Personal branding as professional liberation.

**Example concept output:**

**CONCEPT 1 — "The Receipts Wall"**

**Headline:** `CREDIBILITY ISN'T GIVEN. IT'S BUILT.`

**Deep Purple (`#563794`):**
`Bold editorial illustration, split composition. Left side: a man in a dark charcoal suit walks casually through an open door, hands in pockets, relaxed — a rubber stamp marked "TITLE" floating near him like an automatic pass. Background is clean white, minimal, effortless. Right side: a Black woman with natural warm brown skin in a golden-yellow blazer and dark trousers stands confidently with arms crossed in front of a closed door. The wall behind her is deep purple (#563794), covered with framed certificates, awards, trophies, and reports in white and black frames. Bold white headline text at top. Editorial illustration style, clean bold lines, graphic novel aesthetic. Characters have realistic natural skin tones — brand colors are used only for environment, never on skin. Dominant palette is deep purple, black, and white.`

This example shows the pattern. Each concept would have all brand color variants following the same structure.

## Key Reminders

- The headline and visual should each be powerful on their own — together they should be devastating
- These images are for LinkedIn — they need to stop a scroll in under 2 seconds
- The visual metaphor does the emotional work; the headline does the intellectual work
- When in doubt, go bolder with the metaphor, not subtler
- Every image should make someone feel something before they've read a single word of the post
